What's broken, and what it costs to fix

Your site is live and taking visitors right now. Below is everything we found, grouped by who it affects. Most of it you can verify yourself in about two minutes on your phone.

What your customers run into

These affect people who are already looking for you.

The homepage is emptyStart here

pelicanrowmarket.com loads a page with no content on it. A visitor sees a menu bar, a blank space, and a footer. There is nothing telling them what you sell, where you are, or when you're open.

The contact page sends people to Wix

The Facebook and Instagram icons point to Wix's own accounts instead of yours. Anyone who clicks them leaves your site and lands somewhere unrelated, on the one page where they'd already decided to reach out.

The map won't give directions

The map on the contact page opens Google generally rather than your pin at 6413 Johnston Street. Customers can't get turn-by-turn directions to the store.

The business isn't on Apple Maps

You have a listing on Google Maps but none on Apple Maps. Most people who hear about Pelican Row and pull out an iPhone will type it into the map app that came with the phone, and right now that search comes up empty. Claiming the listing puts your name, address, hours and pin in front of them, and also feeds Siri and the built-in search on those phones.

Site search returns unfinished pagesStart here

Searching the site surfaces blank template pages left behind by apps that were installed but never set up. A customer looking for a vendor or an item gets a list of empty back-end screens instead.

The footer reads © 2035

A leftover from the template. It reads as an unfinished site, which makes people hesitate before trusting the business behind it.

No icon in the browser tab

The site still shows the generic Wix logo. Anyone with your page open beside a few others can't pick you out.

What Google sees

These affect whether people find you in the first place.

No description on any page

Search results show your page title and nothing underneath. Competitors get two lines to make their case; you get none.

Shared links show up blank

Post the link on Facebook and it appears with no photo and no description. There's no preview image set anywhere on the site, so every share looks like a dead link.

Pages are named blank, blank-1, blank-2

Your Privacy Policy, Shipping, Terms, Refund and Accessibility pages all still carry placeholder web addresses. Those are the addresses Google indexes and customers see.

The business name isn't consistent

The site identifies itself as "Pelican Row Estate" in some places and "Pelican Row Estate & Market" in others. Search engines treat inconsistent naming as a signal that they're looking at different businesses.

Hours of operation aren't listed

Your address appears on the site, but store hours don't. For a walk-in business, that's the question customers ask before they get in the car, and it's one of the first things Google looks for.

Your Trust Acadiana listing is filed under Embroidery

The listing itself is good, with a strong description and photos. But it sits in the directory under Advertising & Marketing, subcategory Embroidery. A Trust member browsing Retail for antiques or furniture to spend Trade Dollars on won't find you. The directory has no antiques or thrift category, so the best fit is Retail, under Misc Retail, with Furniture and Home Goods added if Trust allows more than one. The directory also doesn't show a website, phone or address for any member, so it's worth working yours into the description itself.

Under the hood

Not visible to customers, but they're the reason for several problems above.

Unused apps are loading on every page

Online Store, Events & Tickets, Loyalty, Rewards, Members Area and Chat all appear in the site's source code and don't appear to be in use. Each one adds public pages and loads its own code on every visit. These are the empty pages showing up in search. We'd keep whichever you plan to use and remove the rest.

No main heading on the homepage

Search engines read the main heading first to understand what a page is about. Yours doesn't have one.

The logo link isn't labeled

Screen readers announce the pelican logo as an unlabeled link. A small fix, and part of keeping the site accessible.

Worth considering

Not problems, but gaps where the site could be doing work it isn't. All of these are included in the new site described below.

A page for prospective vendors

There's nothing on the site aimed at someone who wants to rent a booth. A page covering what's available, what it costs, and how to get in touch would let the site recruit for you instead of leaving it to word of mouth and the sign out front.

Your vendor agreement, available to read first

Posting the agreement as a download lets a prospective vendor review the terms before they come in. It answers the questions you'd otherwise field by phone, and the people who do show up have already decided.

Vendor hours listed separately from store hours

If vendors can get to their booths to restock outside of retail hours, that's worth stating. It's a selling point against markets that don't allow it, and it saves you explaining it every time someone asks.

An Events tab

Sidewalk sales, vendor markets, seasonal openings, anything that brings people out. A dedicated tab in the menu with a calendar behind it gives customers a reason to check back. The events tools are already installed on the site, so this is a matter of setting them up rather than adding something new.

A vendor waiting list

When every booth is taken, the site should still capture the people who wanted one. A short form that collects a name, contact and what they sell gives you a list to call the day a space opens up, instead of starting from zero.

An About Us page

Who runs the market, how it started, and what kind of place it is. People buying antiques and secondhand goods like to know who they're buying from, and it's the page Google reads to decide the site belongs to a real business.

A vendor spotlight for featured and new vendors

A rotating feature on the homepage: the vendor of the month, plus a welcome for anyone who's just moved in. It gives regulars a reason to come see what's new and gives your vendors something to share with their own followers. The Booth of the Month program below is built to keep this fed.

A policy page

One plain-language page stating that all sales are final, along with how you handle holds, damage and anything else that comes up at the register. Stating it up front heads off disputes, and it replaces the placeholder policy pages the site has now. The questionnaire collects what we'd need to write it.

Investment

Everything above could be patched one item at a time inside Wix. We don't recommend it. You'd still be on the platform that produced these problems, and none of the things you've told us you want to add, from vendor listings to a waiting list to an events tab, are things Wix does well. So we're proposing a new site instead, in two pieces: the first replaces the site, the second keeps it running and worth visiting.
